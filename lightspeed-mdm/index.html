Your child's device at AISA

Your child's device at AISA

What Lightspeed device management means for your family — and what you need to do before term starts.

Welcome parents. Set the tone early: this is a short session, we will explain exactly what is being installed, and there is time for questions at the end. Nobody should leave this room unsure about what the school can and cannot see.

The short version

If you remember one thing

The management rules apply during the school day, and only while the device is on the AISA network. Outside those hours, or off our Wi‑Fi, the device behaves exactly as it did before.

Both conditions have to be true at the same time. At home on a weekend, or on your own mobile data, nothing is filtered and nothing is logged.

This is the single most important slide. Say it slowly, and say it twice. Most worry in the room comes from parents assuming this is always-on monitoring of a device they paid for. It is not.

Plain language

What is a management profile?

Lightspeed is the system AISA uses to manage devices for learning. When your child's device is enrolled, it receives a management profile — a small settings file that tells the device which rules to follow while it is being used for school.

It is not an app that watches the screen, and it is not antivirus. Think of it as the school's set of classroom rules, written in a form the device understands.

Being straight with you: the profile stays on the device all the time — it does not uninstall itself at 3pm. What changes outside school hours is that its rules stop applying. We would rather you heard that from us now than discovered it later.

Expect the question "so it is always installed?" — answer it before it is asked, as written here. Honesty on this point buys credibility for everything else.

Why we're doing this

The reasons behind the change

  • Keeping children safe online at school. While your child is learning on our network, we have a duty of care to filter harmful content. This is how we meet it.
  • A calmer classroom. Teachers can keep the class on the task in front of them instead of policing tabs and games.
  • Fair access for everyone. School apps and resources can be delivered to every device, so no child is stuck because something would not install.
  • Less admin for families. School apps and Wi‑Fi settings arrive automatically, rather than you setting them up by hand.

Lead with safeguarding, close with the benefit to the family. If asked whether this is required by ADEK or another authority, do not guess — take the question and follow up in writing.

Walk the grid cell by cell. Land hard on the bottom row: a sick day at home, homework in the evening, the whole summer — none of it is managed. Confirm the exact school-hours window with IT before presenting.

The detail

What it does, and what it doesn't

While managed, the school can

  • Filter websites that are not appropriate for school
  • Install and update the apps used for lessons
  • See which school sites were visited during lessons
  • Apply different rules by year group

The school cannot

  • Read your family's personal files, photos or messages
  • Switch on the camera or microphone
  • Track where the device is
  • See anything at all outside school hours or off our network

Every flagged line on this slide must be confirmed by IT against the actual Lightspeed configuration before this is shown to parents. Do not soften or expand these claims from the stage — if you are unsure, say you will confirm in writing.

Your part

What you need to do

  1. Bring the device to school

    Your child brings their device on the date given for their year group.

  2. We enrol it

    Enrolment takes a few minutes and is done at school by the IT team. No software of your own is removed.

  3. Check it still works the way you expect

    Take it home, use it as normal that evening, and tell us if anything behaves differently. Off our network, it should feel unchanged.

Give the real dates per year group here. Parents want a date and a location more than they want reassurance at this point in the session.

The device is yours

When your child leaves AISA

It is your device, and it stays your device. When your child leaves the school, the management profile is removed and the device returns to being completely unmanaged.

If you need the profile removed sooner — because you are selling the device, or your child is changing schools — contact the IT team and we will remove it.

This slide matters more than its length suggests. "Can I get it off?" is the question parents most often leave unasked and worry about afterwards.

Questions we're asked

Common questions

Can the school see what my child does at home?

No. The rules only apply during school hours while the device is on the AISA network. At home — on your Wi‑Fi or mobile data — nothing is filtered and nothing is recorded.

Do I get a login to see my child's activity?

Not at this stage. Lightspeed does offer a parent portal, but AISA is not switching it on for now, so there is no parent login to set up. If that changes we will tell you before it does.

Can I refuse?

Speak to us. A device used for learning at AISA needs to be enrolled, but if you have concerns we would rather have the conversation than have you worry.

Will it slow the device down or use up storage?

No. A management profile is a small settings file, not a running program.

What about the rest of the family on that device?

If the device is shared, the school rules still only apply during school hours on our network — so ordinary family use in the evening is unaffected. We recommend a separate user account for your child where the device supports it.
